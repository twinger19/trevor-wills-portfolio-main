Designing Headwear and Gloves for Levi’s and Dockers

Belts and wallets were only part of the job. I also designed headwear and gloves for Levi’s and Dockers, from baseball caps and ivies to trappers, beanies and leather gloves, and took each style from concept CAD through tech pack and factory sample.

The work

Fall headwear and glove lines for two Levi Strauss & Co. brands, made to sell next to our belts, wallets and bags.

01 / The stories

Every season started with a story

Each Dockers season was built around a few stories. Big Sur and Lake Tahoe set the colors, materials and mood, and every hat and glove in the line had to belong to one of them.

The story boards opened the line book at the first review with the brand, so the line read as a point of view before anyone saw a single hat.

02 / One hat, start to finish

The herringbone ivy, from CAD to sample

The Dockers Wool Herringbone Flat Top Ivy shows how a style moved through development. It started as a CAD in four colorways for the first review. The tech pack then set the crown fabric and lining for each colorway, the debossed rubber logo patch, and the twill tape and sweatband branding inside the hat. Each colorway was matched to herringbone swatches from our mills, with a note to the factory to source those qualities or similar at the right cost. By the Mid Point review, the CADs were real samples.

03 / The range

Caps to trappers

Across the two brands, the lines covered baseball and camp caps, ivies, fedoras, bucket hats, trappers and beanies. Levi’s leaned into washed denim, corduroy and patches. Dockers stayed dressier, with wool plaids, herringbones and tweeds.

04 / Gloves

Gloves, from knit to leather

Gloves sat in the same cold weather line, so they shared its materials: wool with leather palms, washed denim with sherpa lining, buffalo plaid with a knit cuff. Each concept sheet is tagged with the factory developing it, which kept the hand-off clear once samples were underway.

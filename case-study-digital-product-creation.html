Bringing 3D sampling to accessories

Leading the company-wide move to 3D sampling, and our first AI tools, in accessories design.

Role

Design Director, 3D Champion

Partner

Target's 3D Product Creation team

Scope

Enterprise rollout · CLO 3D

Impact

−66% samples · −2 wks cycle

Retail partners like Target were moving to 3D sampling in every department. It was standard in apparel, but almost nobody was doing it in accessories, especially mixed soft and hard goods like belts.

The pressure came from two sides. Sampling costs and timelines were climbing, and retailers were asking for digital workflows we didn’t have yet. There was a sustainability cost too: every round of strike-offs, prototypes and revisions uses material and ships across the world, and that adds up across hundreds of SKUs.

My mandate was to research and choose the right software, get the team proficient, set up an international 3D team, and meet our retail partners’ expectations in a category with no playbook.

The vision & solution

I put together a trial group of designers to test 3D software, working directly with Target’s 3D Product Creation team. Together we chose CLO 3D because it could handle mixed-material accessories. I hired a full-time 3D designer from one of our China offices, and we built the standards and workflow for 3D in our category.

At the same time, I worked with VPs across product development, operations and IT to get buy-in for changing how the whole company developed product. Physical samples dropped 66%, which meant fewer materials ordered, fewer prototypes shipped overseas and a lighter development footprint.

Test new tools with a small group, keep designers in charge of the decisions, and scale what works.

How I bring in new tools

Bringing in AI

As a founding member of RAA’s AI Trailblazers group, I brought generative AI tools like Vizcom.AI, image generation and research workflows into trend research, concept work and design. I used the same approach as with 3D: start small, protect the designer’s judgment, and roll out what proves itself.

The rollout also fed into the sustainability work I led as Product Group Lead on RAA’s ESG Committee. Cutting samples turned out to be both the responsible choice and the cheaper one.
